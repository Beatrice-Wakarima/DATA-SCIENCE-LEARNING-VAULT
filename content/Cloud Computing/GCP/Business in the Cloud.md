

## On-Premises vs Cloud

- **On-Premises** → businesses own and maintain hardware/software in their facilities.
    
    - Full control over IT environment.
        
    - Higher **CapEx** and operational demands.
        
- **Cloud Infrastructure** → hosted/managed by third-party provider.
    
    - Flexibility and scalability.
        
    - Reduced upfront costs.
        
    - Less control over physical IT resources.
        

## Financial Model

- Cloud shifts costs from **CapEx → OpEx**.
    
- Subscription or pay-as-you-go basis.
    
- Benefits:
    
    - Scale resources up/down as needed.
        
    - Lower **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)** if managed properly.
        
    - Eliminates hardware maintenance, reduces IT staffing needs, improves efficiency.
        
- Risk: operational expenses can accumulate if not monitored.
    

## Responsibility

- On-premises → business responsible for all infrastructure, updates, and security.
    
- Cloud service models distribute responsibilities differently:
    
    - **IaaS** → provider manages physical infrastructure; customer manages OS/apps.
        
    - **PaaS** → provider manages infrastructure + OS/middleware; customer manages apps/data.
        
    - **SaaS** → provider manages entire stack; customer manages only data/user access.
        

## IaaS

- Flexibility and control over infrastructure.
    
- Reduces TCO by eliminating hardware investments.
    
- Requires in-house technical expertise.
    
- Best for companies needing customization.
    

## PaaS

- Focus on application development and management.
    
- Lower TCO by reducing infrastructure management.
    
- Suitable for developers building apps without platform maintenance.
    
- Less control compared to IaaS.
    

## SaaS

- Provider manages everything (apps → hardware).
    
- Lowest TCO, minimal effort.
    
- No need for specialized IT staff.
    
- Least flexibility/control, but most user-friendly.
    
- Businesses focus on using software rather than managing it.
